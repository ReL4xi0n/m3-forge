## Summary

Building for all is M3's inclusive-design process guidance: design with the people a product
serves, not only for them. It has two parts. User needs: widen whose experience you consider.
Co-design: bring those people into the work early and keep them there. It sets no visual rules;
it shapes how research, testing and decisions are done.

## Rules

### Understand whose needs you are meeting

- A product team rarely represents all its users. Close the gap by working with communities,
  advocacy organizations, subject experts and researchers, and look hardest for the perspectives
  that usually get missed.
- Use the dimensions of human experience as a checklist during research, design, testing and
  marketing. The list is open-ended; grouped here:
  - body and ability: disability, physical attributes, age;
  - identity: gender, sexual orientation, race, ethnicity, religion, culture;
  - circumstances: socioeconomic status, geography and global relevance, education and literacy,
    comfort with technology.
- Considering these helps you account for varied lived experience, find the right people to
  collaborate with, and extend the product's benefits to more people.

### Co-design

- Co-design goes beyond ordinary user research: the people who will use the product take part in
  creating it.
- Engage people of varied backgrounds, especially from overlooked communities, and treat it as
  continuous work, not a one-off study.
- Test assumptions with market research across a broad range of people.
- Questions to ask the team at each stage:
  - Who else could benefit, and what do they need?
  - Are overlooked communities part of ongoing testing, and are community members and experts
    involved early and repeatedly?
  - How does their feedback change feature priorities, functionality and messaging?
  - How do insights carry from ideation into later stages, and where could you learn more?
  - What harm could follow from leaving people out, and how will you prevent it?
  - If the product uses ML or AI, how will you reduce the risk of bias?

## Accessibility

- Disability is one dimension among many here; the accessibility principles are in
  `overview.md`, and text-specific requirements are in `writing.md`.
- Include disabled people as co-designers, not only as test participants at the end.
- Treat the dimension list as a prompt for recruiting research participants, not a box-ticking
  exercise.

## Platform mapping

Not covered by the sources. The guidance is platform-neutral and has no component or API.

## Pitfalls

- Assuming the team's own experience stands in for its users'.
- Consulting communities once, late, and only to validate a finished design.
- Collecting feedback that never changes priorities or messaging.
- Shipping ML or AI features without checking for bias against the groups above.
